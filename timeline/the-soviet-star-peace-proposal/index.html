The Soviet "Star Peace" proposal

1985

In August 1985 Soviet Foreign Minister Eduard Shevardnadze asked the United Nations to take up "international co-operation in the peaceful exploration of outer space under conditions of its non-militarization." The Soviet plan called on states to refrain from developing, testing and deploying space strike weapons, to build major international orbital scientific stations together, and to establish a world space organization once space had been kept free of weapons. Presenting it to the General Assembly on September 24, Shevardnadze contrasted the "sinister plans of Star Wars" with a concept of "Star Peace." The State Department said it saw no link between the proposal and U.S. research on strategic defenses, and at the Soviet Union's request no vote was taken on its draft resolution. It was the Soviet counterpart to Reagan's Strategic Defense Initiative, and the next year at Reykjavik Mikhail Gorbachev insisted that SDI research be confined to laboratories. 1,2,3,4
