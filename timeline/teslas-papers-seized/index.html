Tesla's papers seized

1943

Two days after Nikola Tesla died in his New York hotel room on January 7, 1943, the FBI had the Office of Alien Property Custodian seize his belongings, worried that plans for his claimed death ray could reach enemies of the United States through his Yugoslav nephew. MIT engineer John G. Trump examined them for three days and found nothing that would "constitute a hazard in unfriendly hands"; a box said to hold part of the death ray contained an old resistance box. His papers are now in Belgrade's Nikola Tesla Museum. 1
