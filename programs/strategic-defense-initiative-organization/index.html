Strategic Defense Initiative Organization

1984 to 1993

United States · 1984 to 1993 · Renamed BMDO in 1993, Missile Defense Agency in 2002

Set up in 1984 within the Department of Defense to develop Ronald Reagan's Strategic Defense Initiative, which he announced on March 23, 1983, as a way of "rendering these nuclear weapons impotent and obsolete." Its first director was Lt. Gen. James Abrahamson, a former director of the Space Shuttle program. It studied lasers, particle-beam weapons and ground- and space-based interceptors, among them Edward Teller's nuclear-pumped X-ray laser, Project Excalibur, at Lawrence Livermore, and Brilliant Pebbles, swarms of small orbiting interceptors conceived in 1986 by Lowell Wood and Teller. 1,2

Critics called it "Star Wars": Missile Defense Agency historians trace the name to a Washington Post article of March 24, 1983, and Sen. Ted Kennedy spoke of "reckless Star Wars schemes." The X-ray laser failed several key tests in 1986, and in 1987 the American Physical Society concluded the technologies were decades away from readiness, after which SDI's budget was cut. At the 1986 Reykjavik summit Gorbachev's demand that SDI research be confined to laboratories helped the talks collapse at the last minute. In 1993 the Clinton administration redirected the work toward theater missile defense and renamed the agency the Ballistic Missile Defense Organization; it became the Missile Defense Agency in 2002. 1,3,4
