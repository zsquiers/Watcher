HMS Caroline lights

South of Jeju Island, Korea, 1893

South of Jeju Island, Korea, 1893 · UAP or anomaly site · 32.967°N, 126.550°E

Captain Charles J. Norcock of HMS Caroline wrote in Nature that at 10 p.m. on February 24, 1893, about 17 miles south of Quelpart, now Jeju Island, his officer of the watch reported globular lights low over the sea, like Chinese lanterns strung between the masts of a tall ship. They held the same bearing until about midnight. The next night, east of Port Hamilton, they appeared again and kept a constant bearing from 10 p.m. until daylight while the ship steamed east, shifting from a cluster into a crescent, a diamond or a curved line. Through a telescope they looked reddish and seemed to give off a thin smoke. He added that HMS Leander had seen lights in the same area that rose higher as the ship turned toward them. 1
