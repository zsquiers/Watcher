Itzhak Bentov

Inventor and consciousness author, 1923 to 1979

Also known as Emerich Tobiás, Ben-Tov

Born in Humenné, Czechoslovakia, he moved to Palestine after World War II, joined Israel's Science Corps, and is credited with designing the country's first rocket for the 1948 war. He emigrated to the United States in 1954, built a steerable heart catheter in 1967, and co-founded Medi-Tech, later bought by Boston Scientific. 1

In Stalking the Wild Pendulum (1977) he wrote that "consciousness permeates everything" and described the body as a system of vibrations, with the heart and aorta resonating at about seven cycles per second. In 1983 an Army officer assessing the Monroe Institute's Gateway Experience drew on his biomedical models. Bentov died in 1979 aboard American Airlines Flight 191 at Chicago. 1,2,3
