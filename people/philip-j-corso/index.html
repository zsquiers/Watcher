Philip J. Corso

Army officer, 1915 to 1998

Also known as Philip Corso

A U.S. Army intelligence officer from California, Pennsylvania, who was commissioned in 1942 and spent ten years as a lieutenant colonel. In May 1961 he joined Army research and development at the Pentagon under Lt. Gen. Arthur Trudeau, where, by his own account, he ran the Foreign Technology desk. He resigned from the Army in 1963 and went to work for Senator Strom Thurmond. 1,2

His 1997 book The Day After Roswell made him one of the best-known Roswell witnesses. The book says he served on President Eisenhower's National Security Council staff, but researcher Kevin Randle, citing the Eisenhower Presidential Library, found no record that he held that role. 1,3
