The Day After Roswell

1997 · Philip J. Corso

Ghostwritten by William J. Birnes and published for the 50th anniversary of Roswell, it claimed that Corso, as an Army officer, quietly fed technology from the crashed craft to U.S. industry. It spent three weeks on the New York Times bestseller list. Senator Strom Thurmond had written a foreword believing it was a plain memoir; once he learned what the book said, he stated he knew of no such cover-up, and his foreword was withdrawn. Reviewers found dates and events in it that could not have happened, and The Guardian later named it one of the top 10 literary hoaxes. 1,2,3,4
