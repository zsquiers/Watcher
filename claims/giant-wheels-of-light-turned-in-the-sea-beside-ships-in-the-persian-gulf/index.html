Giant wheels of light turned in the sea beside ships in the Persian Gulf

Claimed by Commander J. Eliot Pringle, and the Patna's officers · 1879 · Reports in Nature (1879) and Knowledge (1883)

Partly confirmed · Some of it is on the record; the rest is not.

The claim

In 1879 and 1880, officers on HMS Vulture and the steamer Patna described huge wheels of light hundreds of yards across, with glowing spokes, turning in the water of the Persian Gulf. Charles Fort argued in 1919 that they were vast wheel-like super-constructions entering the ocean. 1,2,3

The official record

The displays are well recorded, and sailors kept reporting similar ones in the Indian Ocean and nearby seas into the 20th century. But Pringle, who saw the Vulture display, found from his masthead that the wheels were really parallel bands of light and the turning an illusion of perspective. The glow is most likely bioluminescence from plankton such as dinoflagellates, which flash when the water is disturbed. What sets off such fast, regular bands is still argued over: ideas include pressure waves from undersea earthquakes, proposed by Kurt Kalle in 1960, and optical effects, with none of them proven. One Knowledge reader answered the Patna letter by suggesting the crew had been drinking. 1,3,4,5
