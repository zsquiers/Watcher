A ground-level light near Haynesville burned the trees with the power of a power plant

Claimed by Louie A. Galloway · 1967 · Report to Project Blue Book, the Condon study, and a 2025 journal paper

Unverified · No public evidence either way.

The claim

Galloway estimated that the light he saw in 1966 put out 400 to 1,200 megawatts, and a 2025 paper by Jacques Vallée and two French colleagues put it at 500 to 1,400 megawatts. Bark from the blackened side of a pine at the clearing, tested at the laboratories of France's Atomic Energy Commission, held cesium-137, while bark from the unexposed side of the same tree did not. 1

The official record

The Condon team flew over the area, searched it with Galloway and found only trees, oil wells and a burned-over oil slick by a pumping station; it called the power estimate dubious, since nothing was known about how the light radiated. The bark was collected after the Condon report, sent to a U.S. atomic facility in 1978 and returned without results, then kept for decades before the 2025 tests. Cesium-137 is also spread everywhere by bomb-test fallout; the paper argues fallout should then show on both samples, but the result rests on two samples from one tree and has not been repeated by others. 1,2
