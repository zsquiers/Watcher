A 4,000-year-old UFO was recovered near Comanche Point in the Grand Canyon

Claimed by Weekly World News, a supermarket tabloid · 1994 · A Weekly World News cover story datelined Flagstaff, Arizona

Shown false · The evidence for it was shown to be fake or mistaken.

The claim

The story said a secret joint military task force on UFOs found a spherical craft, 102 feet long, lodged in limestone rubble at the base of the canyon near Comanche Point. Carbon dating supposedly showed it had crashed about 4,000 years ago, around 2000 B.C., and nearby Indian cave paintings were said to show humanoids with bulbous heads. A California astronomer named Dr. Henry Leaumont was quoted, and the craft was said to have been moved to a secret location. 1

The official record

The only source is the Weekly World News cover story of September 27, 1994. French researcher Patrick Gross, who archived the text, notes that the tabloid was known for making up such stories and that no evidence is provided and that carbon dating works only on organic remains, not metal. Wikipedia's article on Comanche Point calls it an outlandish cover story. 1,2
