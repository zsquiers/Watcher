The Bluegill Triple Prime blast knocked down a non-human craft

Claimed by Geoff Cruickshank · 2024 · Liberation Times article and an interview with Ross Coulthart

Disputed · Officials and the claimant give conflicting accounts.

The claim

Geoff Cruickshank argues that declassified films of the October 1962 test show a smoking object tumbling away from the fireball, which he believes was a craft of non-human origin, and that a white triangle on one film was added to hide it. He ties the two films to KETTLE 1 and KETTLE 2, instrumented KC-135 planes that filmed the blast, though his accounts have differed on which shows which. He also points to ship logs and high radiation readings among recovery crews. 1,2,3,4

The official record

No official body has addressed the claim. Researcher Douglas Dean Johnson traced the films through the declassified test reports and concluded the falling object is very likely the 65-foot Thor booster that carried the warhead, which a 1960s report describes as a small white dot below the burst; he traced the falling-object films to KETTLE 2, and the triangle film to a ground camera on Johnston Island rather than either plane. Nuclear-film experts he consulted said the triangle was on the original film, from an object inside the camera, not a redaction mask. The high radiation readings turned up on 16 ships and were blamed at the time on faulty seals in the badge film packs. 2,5
