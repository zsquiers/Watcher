The Hutchison effect levitates objects and fuses metal

Claimed by John Hutchison · 1979 · Videos and demonstrations in his apartment laboratory

Unverified · No public evidence either way.

The claim

Hutchison says that interacting electromagnetic fields from his equipment make heavy objects rise, cause metal to fracture or become jelly-like, and fuse dissimilar materials such as metal and wood. He says he has demonstrated the effect for scientists from U.S. Army intelligence. 1

The official record

The effect has not been reproduced by anyone else, or by Hutchison in front of unbiased observers. After a video of a toy UFO showing a visible string, he first said it was a wire in the apparatus, then admitted he had been "creative" with the footage. Skeptics suggest an electromagnet on the ceiling, hidden pieces of metal and an upside-down camera, and note that the videos do not show what happens to the objects after they rise. No source confirms the Army demonstration or any military funding. 1,2
