The 1971 nuclear-accidents agreement shows the superpowers took UFOs seriously

Claimed by UAP researchers, including a 2025 review led by Kevin Knuth · 2025 · Books, articles and a 2025 scientific review

Partly confirmed · Some of it is on the record; the rest is not.

The claim

UAP researchers point to Article 3 of the 1971 agreement, in which the United States and the Soviet Union pledged to tell each other when their missile warning systems detected unidentified objects. A 2025 review by Kevin Knuth and 33 co-authors says that, without naming any cause, it shows a mutual concern from the early 1970s that an unknown object or interference could lead to a missile being fired by mistake. 1,2

The official record

The clause is real, and it does show that both sides feared a misread warning could start a nuclear war. But it says nothing about what such objects might be: "unidentified" covers anything the warning systems could not identify, and the same agreement also covers accidental nuclear detonations and advance notice of missile tests. It came out of working groups on accidental war attached to the SALT arms talks, not out of any UFO study. 1,3
