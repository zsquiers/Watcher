Roswell technology was secretly fed to U.S. industry

Claimed by Philip J. Corso · 1997 · The Day After Roswell (book) and TV interviews

Disputed · Officials and the claimant give conflicting accounts.

The claim

Philip Corso wrote that wreckage from the 1947 Roswell crash sat in Army files until 1961, when, running the Foreign Technology desk of Army research and development, he quietly passed pieces to defense contractors. He said this seeded advances such as microcircuits, lasers, fiber optics and the microwave oven. 1

The official record

No document supports his role, and each of those technologies has a well-documented development history. Reviewers found impossible events in the book, such as a 1961 confrontation with CIA official Frank Wisner at Langley, when Wisner was posted in London and the Langley headquarters had not yet opened. Senator Strom Thurmond, who wrote the foreword, said he knew of no such cover-up, and a 1997 British defence memo released in 2011 dismissed Corso for a record of unreliable testimony. 1,2,3
