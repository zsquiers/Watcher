Three glowing bodies rose out of the sea beside the brig Victoria

Claimed by The Victoria's crew, via the Malta Mail · 1845 · Newspaper report, reprinted by the British Association

Unverified · No public evidence either way.

The claim

A Malta newspaper reported that on June 18, 1845, the becalmed brig Victoria lost her upper masts as if to a sudden squall, that the air turned hot and smelled of sulphur, and that three luminous bodies then rose from the sea half a mile away and stayed in sight for about ten minutes. 1

The official record

The account reached print only through the newspaper. The British Association filed it in its catalogue of luminous meteors with reports of a great meteor seen that evening from Mount Lebanon and from near Adalia, where the Rev. F. Hawlett watched a fireball break into glowing pieces that lingered for 20 minutes to half an hour. A bright fireball low on the horizon can seem to rise out of the sea, though that would not account for the lost masts. Charles Fort retold the story in 1919, and it has never been checked further. 1,2
