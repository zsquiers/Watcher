The green fireballs were not natural meteors

Claimed by Lincoln LaPaz · 1949 · The 1949 Los Alamos conference

Officially explained · An official investigation gave an ordinary explanation.

The claim

Lincoln LaPaz, who had studied meteors for years, argued that the green fireballs seen over New Mexico from late 1948 flew too flat, glowed too green, and left no fragments where they should have landed. He suggested they were artificial, perhaps Soviet spy devices. 1,2

The official record

Others at the conference pointed out that green meteors, though uncommon, had been recorded before, that a meteor can look flat from some angles, and that an unusually clear winter explained the many sightings. The Air Force's Project Twinkle was set up to settle it but photographed nothing, and its 1951 final report found no sign the fireballs were anything but natural. Edward Ruppelt, who later ran Blue Book, wrote that he still didn't know what they were. 1,3
