The Moon is hollow, or partly hollow

Claimed by Hollow Moon and Spaceship Moon theorists · 1970 · A 1970 Soviet magazine article, later books and websites

Shown false · The evidence for it was shown to be fake or mistaken.

The claim

Believers often quote geophysicist Gordon MacDonald, who in 1962 is said to have written that the data require the Moon's interior to be less dense than its outer parts, and that "it would seem that the moon is more like a hollow than a homogeneous sphere." They also point to the Moon's low average density, 3.3 grams per cubic centimeter against Earth's 5.5, and to NASA's report that when Apollo 12 deliberately crashed its spent lunar module into the surface in November 1969, the Moon rang "like a bell" for almost an hour. In 1970 two Soviet writers, Michael Vasin and Alexander Shcherbakov, went further and suggested in Sputnik magazine that the Moon is a spaceship built by alien intelligence. 1,2

The official record

The MacDonald line is usually traced to a July 1962 article in Astronautics, but the exact wording varies between retellings, and he was reasoning from the limited data on the Moon's motion available before any spacecraft landed. He was a university geophysicist at UCLA, not a NASA employee. In 1965 astronomer Wallace Eckert got a similar "hollow" result from orbital data and rejected it as absurd, and by 1968 better methods had pinned down how the Moon's mass is spread. Laser ranging to reflectors left by Apollo puts its moment of inertia factor at 0.394, very close to a solid ball of even density, and Apollo seismometers show a crust about 45 km thick, a thick mantle, and a small core about 330 km in radius. The long ringing reflects differences in the texture, type and density of lunar rock, and the low average density reflects the Moon's lack of a large iron core like Earth's. 1,2,3
