Globes of light paced HMS Caroline across the sea all night

Claimed by Capt. Charles J. Norcock · 1893 · Letter in Nature

Unverified · No public evidence either way.

The claim

Norcock wrote that over two nights in February 1893, off southern Korea, a group of reddish globes of light hung low over the sea and kept a constant bearing for hours as his ship steamed east, changing formation as they went. 1

The official record

Norcock ruled out fires on shore and a distant volcano and leaned toward St. Elmo's fire, while noting that the mirage that night was extraordinary. A Japanese newspaper he read tied the lights to the "unknown light of Japan" that fishermen saw on cold, clear winter nights. No one investigated at the time, and the lights have never been identified. 1
