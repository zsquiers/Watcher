Lights and color changes appear on the Moon's surface

Claimed by Astronomers and amateur observers over several centuries · 1968 · NASA technical report R-277 (1968), which catalogued the reports

Contested · An official finding that critics reject.

The claim

Observers have long reported a short-lived change in light, color or appearance on the lunar surface: glows, red or pink patches, and obscured detail. Patrick Moore coined the term transient lunar phenomena, and at least one third of the reports come from around the Aristarchus plateau. In 1963 two observers at Lowell Observatory reported red, orange and pink colors there. 1

The official record

Some activity is real: Lunar Prospector detected radon-222 escaping near Aristarchus, and meteorite impacts flash briefly when they hit. But most of the old reports lack the control experiments needed to rule out Earth's atmosphere, the telescope or the observer, and scientists still argue over how often outgassing really happens. None of it points to anything artificial. 1,2
