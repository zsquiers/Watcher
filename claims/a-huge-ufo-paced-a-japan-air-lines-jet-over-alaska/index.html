A huge UFO paced a Japan Air Lines jet over Alaska

Claimed by Captain Kenji Terauchi and crew · 1986 · Pilot report and FAA investigation

Disputed · Officials and the claimant give conflicting accounts.

The claim

On November 17, 1986, Captain Kenji Terauchi of a Japan Air Lines cargo 747 reported three objects flying alongside his plane over eastern Alaska: "two small ships and the mother ship," the largest twice the size of an aircraft carrier. He described rapid movements and sudden stops. Anchorage controllers advised "evasive action," yet he said the lights stayed with the plane as it turned and descended, and early news reports said both the FAA and the Air Force had picked the objects up on radar. 1,2

The official record

On review, the military radar images were dismissed as clutter and the FAA's return as a split image of the plane itself, and two nearby aircraft, a United airliner and an Air Force C-130, saw nothing visually or on radar. The FAA found the crew "normal, professional, rational." Philip Klass noted that Jupiter and Mars were where Terauchi saw lights, that his accounts changed in the retelling, and that the FAA called him a "UFO repeater" whose January 1987 sighting in the same area was traced to village lights shining through ice-crystal clouds. Skeptics Robert Sheaffer and Brian Dunning call it an unevidenced anecdote. 1
