Operation Highjump was driven off by flying saucers from a Nazi base

Claimed by Ernst Zündel's 1970s books and later writers · 1975 · Books, websites and videos since the 1970s

Shown false · The evidence for it was shown to be fake or mistaken.

The claim

The story holds that Byrd's 1946–47 expedition was a military assault on a secret Nazi base in New Swabia, that flying saucers rose to meet it and destroyed several American planes, and that the fleet retreated early. Byrd's March 1947 interview, quoted as warning of objects that could fly from pole to pole at incredible speeds, is offered as his admission. The story was spread by the 1975 book UFO's: Nazi Secret Weapon? by "Mattern Friedrich"; Friedrich was the neo-Nazi publisher Ernst Zündel. 1,2,3,4

The official record

Highjump's own records show one plane lost, the George 1 flying boat, which crashed in a blizzard, and four deaths, all from accidents. Its main base was on the Ross Ice Shelf, on the opposite side of the continent from New Swabia, and it withdrew as winter came early. In the interview, Byrd was warning that a future enemy could bomb the United States by flying over the poles, not describing anything he met; the sensational wording comes from a loose translation of the Spanish. Zündel, a Holocaust denier, is reported to have privately admitted his saucer books were a publicity hoax. 1,2,3,4,5,6
